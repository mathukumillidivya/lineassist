The call LineAssist gets wrong

All three of its failures are the same call, and it is a rule violation.

The line was suspended because its contract had ended. The provider's policy says an agent must not lift that suspension. LineAssist took payment for an overdue bill and reactivated the line anyway.

The fix is to enforce the rule in code, the way the line check already works. It has not been applied: changing the agent after seeing the test calls would make these results dishonest.

What these results do not show

  • New combinations, not new problems. The test calls mix the same kinds of fault as the development calls, in different sets, on the same customer account.
  • The customer is simulated. It always has its phone, always cooperates in the end, and never misreads a screen.
  • Not comparable to the public leaderboard. Both agents talked to a lower-cost customer simulator than published results use. Only the comparison between the two agents holds.
  • One model. Every call used Claude Haiku 4.5. A stronger model would start from a higher baseline.
  • The four layers are not separated. They were added one after another and never removed one at a time, so the share each contributes is not isolated.

Line check

Second model call

Hand-off guard

If the draft passes the customer to a human, a reviewer lists checks not yet done. Any left, and the transfer is withheld. Three overrules at most.

Why the standard agent failed

From its 52 failed development calls.

  • It gave up early. 40 failures were hand-offs to a human while a fix was still available.
  • It worked on the wrong line. In 39 it never looked at the customer's own line, and in 26 it read or changed someone else's.
  • It skipped the data allowance. It missed the data top-up in 34 of the 38 calls that needed one.
  • It invented menus. It described buttons it could not see, and the customer then reported doing things that never happened on the phone.

Development, in the order it happened

Calls solved out of 74 development calls, one attempt each. Single runs on the calls the agent was designed against, so they flatter it and each step is noisy.
